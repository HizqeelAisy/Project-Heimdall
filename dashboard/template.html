      Inside the model

      The forecast above comes from a LightGBM model: 1,889 small decision trees, each trained to fix the mistakes of the trees before it. Choose how many trees to use and watch this day's forecast take shape.

      What it relies on

      One tree, in plain words

      1. Was load at this hour last week below normal?
      2. Was load yesterday, an hour earlier, below normal?
      3. Was load at this hour two weeks ago below normal?
      4. Is it a holiday? …and a few more questions, then a small adjustment.

      The forecast is the sum of 1,889 such adjustments. Run it yourself in notebooks/show_me_the_model.ipynb.

      The evidence, across 99 buildings

      Commercial buildings from the Building Data Genome Project 2, in Austin and Tempe, the closest hot climates in open data. Trained on 2016, tuned on January to March 2017, tested on April to December 2017.

      What the prototype assumes

      • US buildings stand in for Malaysian ones. The model retrains on any building's meter data; the next step is a local pilot.
      • Hourly readings approximate TNB's 30-minute demand intervals.
      • Weather is treated as known a day ahead. Real forecasts carry some error.
      • Managers follow every plan. In an eased hour chiller load drops by 10%, moved into the six hours before; pre-cooling wastes 15% extra energy, charged at the off-peak rate. No comfort limits are modelled.
      • Under the general tariff, where every hour counts, there is no off-peak window to shift into, so Heimdall is built for Time-of-Use buildings.
      • Tariff rates are from vendor guides to TNB's July 2025 schedule. TNB states that maximum demand in the off-peak period is not charged.
